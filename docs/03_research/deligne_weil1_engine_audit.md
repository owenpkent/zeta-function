## 1. Why this audit

Over $\mathbb{F}_q$ the RH for varieties now has three proof shapes: Weil's (Hodge index, form-shaped), Stepanov-Bombieri's (auxiliary functions and degree counts, counting-shaped), and Deligne's Weil I (tensor-power amplification, positivity-of-squares-shaped). The project has mapped where the first walls over $\mathbb{Z}$ (M4: LEARNINGS #128, #131, #132) and where the second walls (R1 + PROP-global: the Stepanov audit). Weil I was noted in both companions only at textbook depth. Its engine matters for a specific reason: its positivity input is coefficient non-negativity of an Euler product (the passive side in the acoustic split #90-94), which EXISTS over $\mathbb{Z}$ unconditionally. So if the amplification road walls, it cannot be at the positivity; the wall must be somewhere the project's map may not yet have a name for. The working hypothesis handed in: over $\mathbb{Z}$ the road tops out at Lindelöf-type statements because $\zeta$ has infinitely many zeros and no bounded-degree rationality, and the audit must decide whether that is a NEW facet or R1 in disguise.

One historical fact frames everything: Grothendieck's intended route to the Weil conjectures was the standard conjectures, i.e. a positivity (the Hodge standard conjecture, M4's $\mathbb{F}_q$ avatar). Deligne bypassed it. The Hodge standard conjecture is STILL open in characteristic $p$ (Milne 2015, p. 42: "In nonzero characteristic, almost nothing is known except for surfaces"; the known cases beyond surfaces are conditional (Milne 2002) or special (Ancona 2021, abelian fourfolds)). So the amplification engine provably does not route through the polarization even at home: it proves RH over $\mathbb{F}_q$ in a world where M4's general form remains unproven there. That is the strongest available certificate that this is a genuinely non-M4 road, stronger than anything checkable for Stepanov.

## 2. The engine skeleton: eight named moves, two amplification loops

Sources: Deligne, *La conjecture de Weil I* (Publ. IHES 43, 1974); Milne, *The Riemann Hypothesis over Finite Fields: From Weil to the Present Day* (arXiv:1509.00797), Section 5, **read in full this session** (text-extracted; the account follows Deligne 1974 and Katz's 1976 PSPUM overview closely and includes Deligne's own testimony from the 2013 Abel interview); Katz 1976 located but not readable (scan, no text layer; flagged in Section 8). The skeleton below is verified against Milne's Main Lemma 5.2, Proposition 5.3, and the hypersurface prototype, with the received sketch corrected where it was wrong (Section 2.1).

- **W1 (FAMILY-EMBED).** Put the variety in a one-parameter family: for the prototype, a general line through $V_0$ in the projective space of degree-$\delta$ hypersurfaces; in general a Lefschetz pencil (SGA 7). The middle cohomology of the fibers is a lisse sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ on an open $U \subset \mathbb{P}^1$.
- **W2 (CUP-PAIRING).** Cup product on fibers gives $\psi: \mathcal{E} \times \mathcal{E} \to \mathbb{Q}_\ell(-d)$, skew-symmetric ($d$ odd), non-degenerate (Poincaré duality), monodromy-invariant. Perfectness only; no signature is used.
- **W3 (BIG-MONODROMY).** The image of $\pi_1(U)$ in $Sp(\mathcal{E}, \psi)$ is Zariski-dense/open (general pencil; Kazhdan-Margulis for Lefschetz pencils). Purpose: makes the coinvariants computable by classical invariant theory of $Sp$, INDEPENDENTLY of the zeros.
- **W4 (RATIONAL-TRACES).** The characteristic polynomials of Frobenius at closed points $u \in U$ have rational coefficients (arranged by induction on dimension: the fiber zeta functions are rational with $\mathbb{Q}$ coefficients and the non-middle cohomology is known). This reality is the precondition for the positivity in W5; it is a hypothesis of the Main Lemma, not free.
- **W5 (RANKIN-SQUARE).** Pass to even tensor powers $\mathcal{E}^{\otimes 2m}$: local traces become $\mathrm{Tr}(\pi_u^n)^{2m} \ge 0$, so $\log Z(U, \mathcal{E}^{\otimes 2m}, T)$ has non-negative coefficients. Positivity is now free, as squares. This is Rankin's 1939 move, imported: Deligne's own account (Abel interview, transcribed in Milne 2015) is that he studied Rankin, "and there I saw that he was using a result of Landau"; per Katz 1976 p. 288 (as quoted by Milne), the trigger was Langlands's 1970 remark (Section 5).
- **W6 (TRACE-FORMULA RATIONALITY + POLE-PINNING).** Grothendieck's theorem: $Z(U, \mathcal{E}^{\otimes 2m}, T) = \dfrac{\det(1 - \pi T \mid H^1_c)}{\det(1 - \pi T \mid (E^{\otimes 2m})_{\pi_1(U)}(-1))}$, a RATIO OF POLYNOMIALS whose pole factor is the monodromy coinvariants. By W3 and $Sp$ invariant theory, $(E^{\otimes 2m})_{Sp} \cong \mathbb{Q}_\ell(-dm)^M$ (all invariants are products of $\psi$-pairings), so Frobenius acts on the pole factor as exactly $q^{md+1}$: the global object's ONLY singularities sit at $|T| = q^{-(md+1)}$, all accounted, at a location linear in $m$.
- **W7 (LANDAU-AMPLIFY, loop 1).** Landau's lemma (a Dirichlet/power series with non-negative coefficients is singular at its abscissa; each local Euler factor is coefficientwise dominated by the global product). Hence every local eigenvalue satisfies $|\alpha|^{2m} \le q_u^{md+1}$, i.e. $|\alpha| \le q_u^{d/2 + 1/(2m)}$. Let $m \to \infty$: $|\alpha| \le q_u^{d/2}$. Duality ($q_u^d/\alpha$ is also an eigenvalue) upgrades to $|\alpha| = q_u^{d/2}$: pointwise purity of the sheaf.
- **W8 (KÜNNETH SELF-POWER, loop 2).** From purity of $\mathcal{E}$, the Main Lemma gives global eigenvalues on $H^1$ only inside the window $q^{d/2 \pm 1/2}$. Deligne closes the half-integer slack with a second amplification on the VARIETY: $\alpha^m$ is an eigenvalue on $H^{dm}(V^m)$ (Künneth), the window applied to $V^m$ gives $|\alpha| \le q^{d/2 + 1/(2m)}$, and $m \to \infty$ plus Poincaré duality pins $|\alpha| = q^{d/2}$ exactly (Milne, Prop. 5.3).

The RH-producing content concentrates in W6: it is the only move that converts an edge-of-convergence statement into the exact location of every eigenvalue, because rationality means all singularities are finitely many and accounted, and big monodromy means the pole budget is computed by symmetry rather than read off the zeros (the K1-cleanness of the proof lives exactly here). W5 + W7 alone are Rankin-Landau, which Rankin had already run over $\mathbb{Z}$ in 1939 with output an exponent saving, not purity (Section 5). Both loops share one mechanism: **the eigenvalue enters with exponent linear in $m$ while the error/pole budget stays bounded or linear with a fixed offset, so dividing by $m$ kills the offset**.

### 2.1 Corrections to the received sketch

The task's sketch was right in outline and wrong in three details worth pinning. (a) "Even tensor powers make positivity free" needs the precondition of RATIONAL (real) traces, W4, a genuine hypothesis arranged by induction. (b) The pole-location statement is not merely "Rankin-Selberg rationality": the load-bearing conjunction is rationality (Grothendieck trace formula) AND the invariant-theoretic computation of the pole factor (W3), which is what keeps the argument non-circular. (c) The bound $|\alpha| \le q^{w/2+1/(2k)}$ arises TWICE, in two distinct loops (W7 at closed points of the family base; W8 on Künneth self-powers of the variety), and "the L-function is a polynomial of bounded degree" is not quite the mechanism: the numerator degree GROWS with $m$; what matters is that the function is rational (all singularities accounted) with the pole exponent linear in $m$. Finitely-many-zeros enters as "a rational function's power series converges up to its nearest pole", not as a fixed degree.

## 4. The wall, named precisely

**The wall is R1 in its trace-formula face (W6), with the PROP-global rider (W1 base, W8 self-products), and it is not M4 and not a new facet.** The three hypotheses, tested:

### 4.1 Is the "infinite degree" wall genuinely new?

No. Over $\mathbb{F}_q$, bounded-degree rationality is not a primitive: it is the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula, i.e. a CONSEQUENCE of Frobenius acting on a finite-dimensional cohomology of the base. Deligne's own testimony locates it there: "the poles were given in various cases quite easily by Grothendieck's theory" (Abel interview, in Milne 2015). Remove the Frobenius-cohomology package and finite-degree-ness falls with it; supply the package and it returns. So "no bounded-degree rationality over $\mathbb{Z}$" is the R1 absence wearing analytic clothes, and the repo's 2026-07-01 R1 update (R1 = the missing operator behind Frobenius) already has the right shape. The refinement the audit adds: the infinite-zero-set over $\mathbb{Z}$ means R1's operator must come with a DETERMINANT structure (nuclear/trace-class per weight, so that $\xi(s)$ is a regularized characteristic polynomial with all singularities accounted), not merely a distributional trace. Every candidate in [`spec_z_cohomology_landscape.md`](spec_z_cohomology_landscape.md) realizes the trace (the explicit formula); none carries the computable-spectrum determinant. That is Deninger's program spec, re-derived from Weil I's engine rather than from the cohomology wish-list.

### 4.2 Does it secretly need M4?

No, and here the certificate is unusually strong. Every positivity the engine consumes was checked: (i) W5's squares of rational traces (SOS, input-side); (ii) W7's Landau domination (coefficient non-negativity of an Euler product, the passive comb); (iii) W2's pairing (perfectness only). All three exist over $\mathbb{Z}$ unconditionally. No move consumes a signature, and the historical record seals it: the engine proved RH over $\mathbb{F}_q$ while the Hodge standard conjecture (M4's $\mathbb{F}_q$ form) remains open there to this day (Milne 2015 p. 42; Ancona 2021 is the state of the art and covers abelian fourfolds only). Purity and polarization split at the theorem level, not just conceptually: Weil I produces facet A (purity) without ever producing facet B (polarization). This is the producer-side complement of the parity audit's consumer-side finding (sieve theory consumes purity moduli, discards signs), and together they harden #130's two-facet reading from both directions.

### 4.3 The single missing structure

The sharpest question posed: what single missing structure would make the $k$-th-moment bound self-improving over $\mathbb{Z}$ the way it is over $\mathbb{F}_q$? Answer, assembled from the table: **a determinant-class trace formula with an independently computable pole budget**. Concretely: a carrier on which $\xi(s)$ is the regularized characteristic polynomial of one operator (zeros = spectrum, all singularities accounted: the W6 rationality clause), equipped with enough symmetry (monodromy/invariant theory, or a functorial tensor structure) that the pole budget of every powered object is computed WITHOUT reading the zeros (the W3 clause, = K1-cleanness), plus a monoidal/Künneth structure to power the carrier (the W8 clause, = PROP-global). Given those, W4, W5, W7 are already present over $\mathbb{Z}$ and the divide-by-$k$ limit would pin every zero: Weil I's argument would run verbatim. The counterfactual makes the accounting exact: the amplification road needs R1 (carrier + trace formula + symmetry) and PROP-global (tensor structure), and needs no polarization at all.

The Lindelöf reading of the wall is then forced rather than hypothesized. With W6 absent, positivity + Landau reach exactly the convergence edge, so the engine's unconditional output type over $\mathbb{Z}$ is: (local) Satake/Ramanujan-type bounds sharpening with each functorial power, and (global) moment bounds whose all-$k$ closure is the Lindelöf hypothesis by definition (Titchmarsh XIII: LH $\iff \int_0^T \lvert\zeta\rvert^{2k} \ll T^{1+\epsilon}$ for all $k$). Lindelöf sits below RH in exactly the measured way: by Backlund's equivalence, LH says the number of zeros with $\mathrm{Re} > 1/2 + \epsilon$ in $[T, T+1]$ is $o(\log T)$: density-zero off-line, never none. An average cannot see an individual zero; the trace formula is precisely the device that converts averages into complete spectral inventories. **The Lindelöf-RH gap is the measured size of the missing W6.** And the one-line form: over $\mathbb{Z}$, GRH implies the Ramanujan bounds (survey-depth claim, via the standard Landau argument on Rankin-Selberg L-functions; cf. Sarnak's GRC notes) but not conversely, so local purity is strictly downstream of zero-location; over $\mathbb{F}_q$, W6 makes the two EQUIVALENT, and that equivalence is the entire difference between the worlds.

## 5. The number-field shadow: the amplification corpus over $\mathbb{Z}$ ran exactly the moves that exist

The engine did not fail to transfer; it transferred move-for-move and its outputs track exactly which moves exist. This section is the literature record the task asked for.

### 5.1 Rankin 1939: the engine's inventor ran it over $\mathbb{Z}$ first

Rankin (1939) formed $F(s) = \sum_n |c(n)|^2 n^{-s}$ for a weight-$2k$ cusp form (W5 over $\mathbb{Z}$, the original), proved meromorphic continuation with singularities only at $s = k$ and $s = k-1$ (a hand-built W6 for the SQUARE only), and applied Landau (W7) to get $|c(n)| = O(n^{k - 1/5})$: an exponent saving, one-fifth of the way from Hecke's trivial bound toward Ramanujan's $n^{k-1/2}$. One power, one saving. The chronology is documented in Milne 2015: Deligne read Rankin (prompted, per Katz 1976 p. 288, by Langlands's remark) and saw that over $\mathbb{F}_q$ "the poles were given... quite easily by Grothendieck's theory" for ALL powers at once. The engine is the same; the function field supplies unlimited W6, the number field supplied it once.

### 5.2 Langlands 1970: the transfer articulated, conditionally, fifty-five years ago

Langlands (*Problems in the theory of automorphic forms*, 1970, pp. 21-22; reproduced in Milne 2015 with the argument spelled out) observed: IF $L(s, \sigma, \pi)$ is analytic for $\mathrm{Re}(s) > 1$ for ALL representations $\sigma$ of the L-group, THEN the generalized Ramanujan conjecture follows, by exactly the W5-W7 loop ($\sigma = \rho \otimes \bar\rho$ gives positive log-coefficients; Landau gives $|\lambda(t_p)|^{2m} \le p$; $m \to \infty$). Milne's gloss: "it is the flexibility of Langlands's construction that makes this kind of argument possible." This is the published statement that over $\mathbb{Z}$ the amplification road's missing input is the pole control of the powered objects, i.e. functoriality, NOT positivity. The audit's W3/W6 rows are Langlands's condition, refactored.

### 5.3 The functoriality ledger: how far the pole-pinning has been built

Jacquet-Piatetski-Shapiro-Shalika (1983) built Rankin-Selberg for $GL(n) \times GL(m)$: the pairs case of W3, with the pole at $s=1$ detecting $\pi' \cong \tilde\pi$. Partial powers: Gelbart-Jacquet ($\mathrm{Sym}^2$), Kim-Shahidi ($\mathrm{Sym}^3$), Kim ($\mathrm{Sym}^4$), yielding Kim-Sarnak $7/64$ toward Ramanujan for $GL(2)$; Luo-Rudnick-Sarnak bounds for $GL(n)$ (survey-depth citations). Newton-Thorne (Publ. IHES 134, 2021, two papers) closed the chain for holomorphic modular forms: $\mathrm{Sym}^n$ is automorphic for all $n$, so the full Langlands amplification now runs for that class. Two honest notes: (a) the proof machinery (automorphy lifting) runs through Galois representations sourced from varieties, so the closure is variety-gated in #130's exact sense; (b) for Maass forms the chain is open, and correspondingly Ramanujan is open: Sarnak's dramatization of R1, reproduced by the amplification ledger. The engine works over $\mathbb{Z}$ wherever W3 has been built, and W3's builders have so far always paid with a variety.

### 5.4 The moments program: the analytic form of the engine and its known ceiling

The $2k$-th moment of $\zeta$ is the $GL(1)$ shadow of the $2k$-th power object (for $\zeta$, the trivial motive, there is no nontrivial $\mathrm{Sym}^k$: the powered object degenerates to $|\zeta|^{2k}$ itself). The ledger: asymptotics proven for $k=1$ (Hardy-Littlewood 1918) and $k=2$ (Ingham 1926; error term Heath-Brown 1979); $k \ge 3$ open. Keating-Snaith (2000) conjectured the constants via random matrix theory; CFKRS (2005) the full polynomial expansions; Diaconu-Goldfeld-Hoffstein (Compositio 139, 2003) recast the moments as multiple Dirichlet series, where the articulated obstruction is precisely the meromorphic continuation of the multi-variable object (proven only slightly beyond absolute convergence: the W6 absence in that program's own language). Upper bounds: Soundararajan (2009) $\ll T(\log T)^{k^2+\epsilon}$ and Harper (2013) $\ll T(\log T)^{k^2}$, BOTH conditional on RH; unconditionally, sharp bounds are known only for $0 \le k \le 2$ (Heap-Radziwill-Soundararajan 2019). Note the direction of the conditionality: the moment bounds FLOW FROM RH, they do not flow toward it. And the program's terminal statement, moments $\ll T^{1+\epsilon}$ for all $k$, is equivalent to Lindelöf (Titchmarsh XIII), which RH implies and which does not imply RH.

### 5.5 The absence finding

No published statement was located this session of the finite-degree obstruction as such: no "amplification cannot exceed Lindelöf over $\mathbb{Z}$ because $\zeta$ is not rational" theorem or slogan, in the moments literature, the subconvexity/amplification literature (Iwaniec-Sarnak, *Perspectives*, GAFA 2000, where "amplification" names the archimedean cousin of the method), or the surveys. The pieces all exist in print separately: the Titchmarsh moment-Lindelöf equivalence (the ceiling as a theorem), Langlands's functoriality-conditional Ramanujan (the missing input named), Deligne's interview (the $\mathbb{F}_q$-specific input named). Nobody has assembled them into the one-line diagnosis this audit reaches (the Lindelöf-RH gap = the missing trace formula). As with the Stepanov audit's van-Frankenhuijsen finding, the assembled statement appears to be genuinely unclaimed territory; unlike there, no single author was found who even frames the question. Absence is a finding.

## 6. Davenport-Heilbronn discipline note

The engine requires an Euler product twice over: W5's squaring squares LOCAL factors (no local factors, no squares), and W7's Landau domination compares local factors against the global series. D-H has a functional equation but no Euler product, so the engine cannot be stated for it: an Architecture-2-style structural exemption, like Stepanov's. But the exemption is NOT wholly hollow here, unlike Stepanov's: the engine's input positivity (coefficient non-negativity of the log of the powered object) is the passive comb, which D-H genuinely FAILS (the project's unconditional passive separation at small $n$, #90-94). So the amplification frame carries a real input-level separator before any construction: D-H is rejected at W5, by computation, not merely by non-mimicry. The honest rider stands anyway: everything ABOVE the comb (the RH-discriminating content) is delegated to the unbuilt W6, so the engine adds no new discrimination leverage on the open kernel; per #131's convention, a frame whose only deep discriminator is unbuilt has not yet entered the discipline's domain at that depth.

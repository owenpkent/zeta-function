## 1. P0 verdict: THEOREM EXISTS, twice over, but neither theorem is the one O3 imagined

**(a) The literal question (BC weights at $\beta \le 1$) is a one-line corollary, because the BC algebra is unital.** Christensen (arXiv:2005.01792, J. Noncommut. Geom. 2023, introduction p. 1) states the governing fact verbatim: "For a unital $C^*$-algebra all KMS weights are on the form $\lambda\omega$ with $\lambda > 0$ and $\omega$ a KMS state," backed in general by his Proposition 3.1 (every KMS weight is finite on a dense subset of the Pedersen ideal; for étale groupoid algebras, finite on all of $C_c(\mathcal{G})$). The BC algebra is unital, so Bost-Connes 1995 (Selecta Math. 1, phase-transition theorem: unique KMS$_\beta$ state for $0 < \beta \le 1$; ergodicity proof in Neshveyev, arXiv:math/0002141) upgrades for free: **for $0 < \beta \le 1$ the KMS$_\beta$ weights of the BC system form exactly one ray $\{\lambda\omega_\beta : \lambda > 0\}$.** Published as a composition, not as a named theorem.

**(b) The consequence the dossier actually needs: O3's non-normalizable-weight phenomenon cannot live on ANY unital ambient.** The dossier's crystal Toeplitz system $\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{N}^\times}$ (Definition 3) is unital, so by the same fact it carries no non-normalizable KMS weight at any $\beta$, for any flow. The divergent-mass crystal ($\sum_n \Lambda(n)/n = \infty$) can only be a weight on a non-unital algebra: the diagonal $c_0(\mathbb{N})$ itself (an infinite Radon measure on discrete $\mathbb{N}$ is legal because $\mathbb{N}$ is non-compact), or a groupoid algebra with non-compact unit space (section 2). Same statement in measure language: Radon measures on a compact unit space are finite, so "weights vs states" buys nothing whenever the unit space is compact (BC's $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is compact; the Nica spectrum of $\mathbb{N}^\times$, $\prod_p \{0, 1, \dots, \infty\}$, is compact). **P0's premise was slightly miscalibrated: the pole = non-normalizability reading needs a non-compact boundary, which is P3's territory.**

**(c) The uniqueness theorem for the dossier's own ambient algebra exists and needs no BC quotient.** Neshveyev-Stammeier (arXiv:1912.03141v3, "The groupoid approach to equilibrium states on right LCM semigroup C*-algebras") work with an arbitrary scale $N: S \to [1,\infty)$ on a right LCM semigroup, dynamics $\sigma^N_t(v_s) = N(s)^{it} v_s$; their introduction names free abelian monoids as the motivating example excluded by the older generalized-scale framework (Afsar-Brownlowe-Larsen-Stammeier, IMRN 2019, arXiv:1611.01052). For $S = \mathbb{N}^\times$, $N(n) = n$ (exactly Definition 3 of the dossier: $\ker N = \{1\}$, $\zeta_N(\beta) = \sum_n n^{-\beta} = \zeta(\beta)$, $\beta_c = 1$):

- **$\beta > 1$:** Theorem 4.5 (read in full): affine homeomorphism between KMS$_\beta$ states on $C^*(S)$ and tracial states on $C^*(\ker N) = \mathbb{C}$. Simplex = one point.
- **All $\beta$:** intro statement of Lemma 5.5 (read; lemma body not read): if $1$ is isolated in $N(S)$ (true for $\mathbb{N}^\times$), any KMS$_\beta$ state is completely determined by its restriction to $C^*(\ker N) = \mathbb{C}$. Hence **at most one KMS$_\beta$ state at every $\beta > 0$, including $\beta = 1/2$.**
- **Existence at $\beta \le 1$:** the Euler product state $\bigotimes_p \mathrm{Gibbs}_{p,\beta}$ on $\bigotimes_p \mathcal{T}_p$ (each factor's partition function $(1 - p^{-\beta})^{-1}$ is finite for all $\beta > 0$); equivalently the product geometric measure on the Nica spectrum satisfies Proposition 3.8's positivity inequalities. This step is my derivation (standard, but I found no sentence in print asserting it for $\mathbb{N}^\times$).
- **Proposition 3.8** (proof read on p. 10 and p. 13): the quasi-invariant probability measure with cocycle $N^{-\beta}$ is unique when it exists, with cylinder values FORCED to $\mu(Z_s) = N(s)^{-\beta}$. For $\mathbb{N}^\times$ that is $\mu(Z_n) = n^{-\beta}$: **the published machinery independently reproduces dossier Lemma 1 (the flat ray is the only quasi-invariant object), and the unique KMS$_{1/2}$ state is the flat/Euler state, not the crystal.** All phase-transition richness in this framework lives in the isotropy fields, never in the measure.

**(d) The Toeplitz-vs-BC distinction, pinned down per the task brief.** Four algebras, four behaviors at $\beta \le 1$:

| Algebra (dynamics from the scale) | $0 < \beta < 1$ | $\beta = 1/2$ status | rich range | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N} \rtimes \mathbb{N}^\times)$ (Laca-Raeburn) | **no KMS states** (stated p. 3: "are no KMS states for $\beta < 1$") | empty | $\beta > 2$: simplex $\cong$ probability measures on $\mathbb{T}$ (via traces on $C^*(\ker N) = C(\mathbb{T})$) | arXiv:0907.3760, Adv. Math. 225 (2010); type III$_1$ on $[1,2]$: Laca-Neshveyev, JFA 261 (2011) 169-187, arXiv:1010.0599 |
| $\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{N}^\times \ltimes \mathbb{Z})$ (Laca-Schulz) | **Bauer simplex**, extreme points $\cong \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ on the whole critical interval $(0,1]$ | NOT a point | $(0,1]$ itself ("supercritical" transition; cyclotomic symmetry breaking) | arXiv:2503.03033 (2025), abstract-only |
| $\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{N}^\times} = C^*(\mathbb{N}^\times) = \bigotimes_p \mathcal{T}_p$ (the dossier's Definition 3) | **exactly one** (NS Lemma 5.5 + product state) | a point | none (no transition at all) | arXiv:1912.03141, Thm 4.5 + Lemma 5.5 |
| BC quotient | **exactly one** ($0 < \beta \le 1$) | a point | $\beta > 1$: extreme points $\cong \hat{\mathbb{Z}}^*$ | Bost-Connes 1995; Neshveyev math/0002141 |

So the task's conditional ("if the Toeplitz-side simplex at $\beta = 1/2$ is not a point, the #82 template needs the BC quotient") resolves: **the Toeplitz-side answer depends on the additive part.** With an additive $\mathbb{N}$: empty. With an additive $\mathbb{Z}$: rich. With no additive part (the dossier's actual algebra): a point, by published machinery, no BC quotient required. The Laca-Raeburn rich simplex the task remembered is at $\beta > 2$ (measures on the circle), not at small $\beta$; the genuine small-$\beta$ richness phenomenon is Laca-Schulz 2025 on the $\mathbb{Z}$-version. Also note Laca-Raeburn Appendix A (p. 3, read): an "ergodicity-free" reconstruction-formula proof of BC uniqueness (Lemma 10.1), the most transport-friendly form of the uniqueness engine.

**K1 tripwire on all imports: CLEAN.** BC uniqueness uses Dirichlet's theorem (primes dense in $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}^*$); NS Thm 4.5/Prop 3.8/Lemma 5.5 are combinatorial-measure-theoretic on the Nica spectrum; Christensen/Neshveyev are general machinery. No hypothesis encodes dilation-system completeness; nothing Nyman-Beurling-shaped is consumed.

## 2. P3 verdict: criterion verified, weights extension verified, and the groupoid question answered in the negative with the failure named

**The criterion.** Neshveyev, "KMS states on the C*-algebras of non-principal groupoids" (arXiv:1106.5912, J. Operator Theory 70 (2013)): for an étale (locally compact, second countable) groupoid $\mathcal{G}$ with dynamics from a continuous cocycle $c: \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{R}$, KMS$_\beta$ states of $C^*_r(\mathcal{G})$ correspond to pairs: a quasi-invariant probability measure $\mu$ on $\mathcal{G}^{(0)}$ with Radon-Nikodym cocycle $e^{-\beta c}$, plus a $\mu$-measurable equivariant field of tracial states on the isotropy group algebras (abstract read; full statement relayed via Christensen, who calls it "Theorem 1.3 in [11]"). **Weights extension: VERIFIED.** Christensen (arXiv:2005.01792, J. Noncommut. Geom. 2023) generalizes Neshveyev's theorem to KMS weights for lc sc Hausdorff étale $\mathcal{G}$ with continuous homomorphism $c: \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{R}$: same data with $\mu$ now a possibly infinite regular (Radon) measure, every KMS weight finite on $C_c(\mathcal{G})$ (Prop 3.1), extremal quasi-invariant measures = ergodic ones (his Thm 5.5). Second countability + local compactness give $\sigma$-compactness, so the dossier's question "is $\sigma$-compact ok" is yes by hypothesis. An earlier partial weights version is Thomsen, arXiv:1306.5118 (two extra conditions on the homomorphism); Christensen notes (p. 2) that prior attempts did not reach full generality. Foundations: Combes 1971 (weights), Kustermans arXiv:funct-an/9704008 (KMS weights), Kustermans-Vaes; modern textbook treatment: Thomsen, "An introduction to KMS weights I+II+III", arXiv:2204.01125.

**The candidate groupoid that gets partway (and is the right home for the weights exemplar).** Take $\mathcal{G}_{\log} = \mathbb{R} \rtimes \mathbb{Q}^*_+$, the transformation groupoid of $x \mapsto x + \log q$ (étale, unit space $\mathbb{R}$ non-compact, action free, isotropy trivial), cocycle $c(x, q) = \log q$. Christensen's theorem applies: KMS$_\beta$ weights $\leftrightarrow$ Radon measures $\nu$ on $\mathbb{R}$ with $(\tau_q)_*\nu = q^{-\beta}\nu$, i.e. $d\nu = e^{-\beta x}\,dm$ with $m$ invariant under the dense subgroup $\log \mathbb{Q}^*_+$. Expected solution set: the single ray $\nu_\beta = e^{-\beta x}dx$ (dense-translate rigidity from $\mathbb{Q}$-linear independence of $\{\log p\}$), which is NON-normalizable: a genuine KMS weight with no state behind it, on a non-compact unit space. This is the formal exemplar the dossier's rows (c)/(e) wanted, and it is exactly the log shadow of the $ax+b$/adele-class boundary. The rigidity lemma itself ("Radon + invariant under a dense subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$ $\Rightarrow$ Lebesgue multiple") is elementary-looking but I did NOT find it stated in this literature: UNCONFIRMED, flagged as the BUILDER target B1 below. Note the crystal comb $\sum_n b_n \delta_{\log n}$ is not quasi-invariant under any $\tau_q$ with cocycle (its failure is dossier Lemma 1 in measure form), so the crystal is again outside the groupoid's KMS cone.

**The assessment: the EF identity is structurally NOT a groupoid cocycle condition.** Two named failures, each precise:

1. **Homogeneity.** For fixed $(\mathcal{G}, c, \beta)$ the KMS-weight set is a cone closed under $\mathbb{R}_+$-scaling (immediate from Christensen Def 2.1: $\lambda\psi$ is KMS if $\psi$ is). The log-crystal set $\mathcal{W}_\zeta$ is NOT: T1 (slope pinned to residue 1, ADVERSARY block 2) makes it an affine slice; the pole + archimedean term of (EF) is inhomogeneous data, and Radon-Nikodym quasi-invariance has no inhomogeneous term to carry it.
2. **Locality/support.** Quasi-invariance is checked bisection by bisection: point-to-point transport along the orbit equivalence relation with strictly positive multiplier $e^{-\beta c}$. (EF) couples the atom at $\log n$ to the entire continuum through the oscillatory Fourier kernel $\cos(r \log n)$: a signed, global, point-to-continuum constraint. It is not supported on any equivalence relation on $\mathbb{R} \sqcup \{\log n\}$ and admits no positive local-transport rewriting.

**Conclusion (the precise negative coordinate P3 asked for):** no étale groupoid over the natural unit spaces has the log-crystal pairs $(\mu, c)$ as its cocycle-quasi-invariant data. The correct formulation is $\mathcal{W}_\zeta = (\text{KMS-weight cone of a groupoid}) \cap (\text{EF slice})$, with ALL rigidity living in the slice; the groupoid layer supplies only the ambient cone and the flat ray. Caveat, stated honestly: this is an obstruction over the natural encodings, not an impossibility theorem over all re-encodings; a re-encoding that made (EF) orbit-local would amount to realizing the explicit formula as an actual flow space, which is the Deninger-Connes geometric frame, i.e. the program's known wall, not a new one. **K1 tripwire for any future P3 work:** the admissible replacement for "Haar on the compact boundary" is dense-translate rigidity of MEASURES (unconditional); upgrading it to density/completeness of the dilation SYSTEM in a function space is Nyman-Beurling and is RH-equivalent. The line sits between measures and function systems; do not cross it silently.

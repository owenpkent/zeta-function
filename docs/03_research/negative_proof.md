## 0. What a disproof of RH is

RH is a $\Pi^0_1$ sentence ([`rh_logical_status.md`](rh_logical_status.md)). Its negation is $\Sigma^0_1$: "there exists a zero $\rho = \beta + i\gamma$ of $\zeta$ with $\beta \neq 1/2$," and such a zero is isolated and certifiable by a finite computation in the ordinary sense (the argument principle on a small rectangle in interval arithmetic; the reverse-mathematics calibration of that counting step is the one item the logical-status document leaves open). A proof of $\neg\mathrm{RH}$ in a $\Sigma_1$-sound theory therefore establishes a true $\Sigma^0_1$ sentence: the witness exists and an unbounded search finds it. The proof itself may be entirely non-constructive (a refuted consequence of RH, an ineffective compactness argument) and may supply no bound on where the zero is. Stated once and used throughout:

**Every negative proof of RH is effectivizable to a number.** It need not be constructive, but it guarantees a witness that search will reach. An analytic disproof mechanism either produces the witness's neighborhood or refutes a consequence of RH; either way, at the end of it there is an off-line zero somewhere, and the only question a mechanism can answer is whether it can say where.

The brute-force branch has been run at scale, here and elsewhere: Platt-Trudgian certify every zero to height $3\times10^{12}$ on the line; this repo certifies the sign of every one of the first 118,488,122 zeros (height $5\times10^7$, [`PRIME_PATTERNS.md`](../../experiments/primes/PRIME_PATTERNS.md)), and the hiding law there shows that a near-critical off-line zero ($\beta = 0.6$) above that height cannot surface in any prime-side observable below $x \sim 10^{150}$ (a zero at $\beta = 0.9$ would need $x \gtrsim 10^{31}$; the threshold is $x^{\beta - 1/2} > \gamma$). So any mechanism has to point above $3\times10^{12}$, and the question this document asks is whether any known mechanism can point anywhere at all.

## 1. The strongest known mechanism: independent phases

Off-line zeros are not exotic. They are proven, in abundance, for every known function that has $\zeta$'s functional-equation shape but is a **sum of at least two Euler products with independent phases**:

- **Davenport-Heilbronn (1936).** $f(s) = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - i\kappa)L(s,\chi) + \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + i\kappa)L(s,\bar\chi)$, $\chi$ the character mod 5 with $\chi(2) = i$. Functional equation of a degree-1 conductor-5 odd character, no Euler product, infinitely many zeros with $\sigma > 1$ (Davenport-Heilbronn, after Potter-Titchmarsh 1935 on Epstein zeta functions), and $\gg T$ zeros in every substrip $1/2 < \sigma_1 < \sigma < \sigma_2 < 1$ (Voronin; Karatsuba-Voronin 1992, Ch. VII). First off-line zero at $0.8085 + 85.699i$ (the repo's control, [`davenport_heilbronn.py`](../../experiments/_shared/davenport_heilbronn.py)).
- **Epstein zeta functions with class number $h(d) \geq 2$** (Potter-Titchmarsh 1935 and Davenport-Heilbronn 1936 II for $\sigma > 1$; Voronin 1976 for the strip). For the principal form $Q_0$ of discriminant $d = d_1 d_2$ with $h = 2$: $Z_{Q_0}(s) = \zeta(s)L(s,\chi_d) + L(s,\chi_{d_1})L(s,\chi_{d_2})$, a sum of two Euler products with the same completed functional equation. The repo's second off-line control ([`epstein_zeta.py`](../../experiments/_shared/epstein_zeta.py); the recorded witness was the NON-principal form of $d = -47$, $h = 5$, whose partial zeta function is a real combination of three Euler products: $\zeta_K$ and the two dihedral weight-1 cusp-form $L$-functions of the class-group characters; probe `e3l`, Session-003 in LEARNINGS). New this session, certified by winding number on discs of radius $0.05$ in the referee's independent Hurwitz-zeta evaluation and re-certified in mpmath at 30 digits: below height 40 the PRINCIPAL form of $d = -15$ has simple off-line zeros at $0.80001 + 12.0386i$, $0.92746 + 15.4966i$, $0.69559 + 20.3460i$, $0.74026 + 33.7569i$ (the first a nine-order cancellation between two Euler products of modulus $\approx 0.62$), and the NON-principal form has one at $0.75807 + 24.4828i$. The Session-003 ledger sentence "$d = 15, 23$ have no off-line zeros at reachable height" was false for both classes, and the module's own zero finder both invented a root (at height 84.76) and missed three; both are corrected in-repo this session (LEARNINGS #217).
- **Hurwitz zeta at rational $a \neq 1/2, 1$** (Davenport-Heilbronn 1936; Cassels 1961 for algebraic irrational $a$): infinitely many zeros with $\sigma > 1$.

The mechanism in all three is one family: Bohr almost-periodicity / Bohr-Jessen value distribution of the summands in the half-plane of absolute convergence (the 1935-36 results), strengthened in the strip by **Voronin's joint universality** (1975): for pairwise non-equivalent Dirichlet characters $\chi_1, \dots, \chi_k$ (the trivial character included, so $\zeta$ counts), the tuple $(L(s + i\tau, \chi_j))_j$ approximates any tuple of functions continuous and nonvanishing on a compact $K \subset \{1/2 < \sigma < 1\}$ with connected complement and analytic inside, uniformly to any $\varepsilon$, for a set of shifts $\tau$ of positive lower density. Given a sum $F = \sum_j c_j L(s,\chi_j)$ with two nonzero coefficients, choose targets whose combination has a simple zero in $K$: $h_2 = -\tfrac{c_1}{c_2}h_1(1 - \epsilon(s - s_0))$ is nonvanishing on the disc $|s - s_0| < 1/|\epsilon|$ while $c_1h_1 + c_2h_2 = c_1 h_1 \epsilon (s - s_0)$ vanishes at $s_0$. Rouché on a smaller disc gives a zero of $F$ in $K + i\tau$ for every good $\tau$: $\gg T$ off-line zeros below height $T$ (positive lower density in the height parameter, which is still a density-zero proportion of all $\sim \tfrac{T}{2\pi}\log T$ zeros).

Call this **the template** $\mathcal{T}$. Its inputs are: (i) a decomposition of the target into $\geq 2$ summands; (ii) joint universality of the summands with independent targets; (iii) Rouché. Its output is $\gg T$ off-line zeros. This is the strongest disproof engine known: it is not heuristic, and it has never failed on an object that satisfies (i)-(ii).

## 2. Aim the template at $\zeta$: three decompositions

$\zeta$ has one Euler product and one phase system. The template needs two. Every attempt to manufacture a second one fails at a nameable joint, and the joint is always a hypothesis of the tool, not a fact about $\zeta$.

**2a. The Hurwitz decomposition (the most literal sum).** $\zeta(s) = q^{-s}\sum_{a=1}^{q}\zeta(s, a/q)$ exactly. Each summand $\zeta(s, a/q)$ with $a/q \neq 1/2, 1$ has infinitely many zeros in $\sigma > 1$ and is universal for targets WITH zeros. So $\zeta$ is a sum of $q$ functions each of which the template kills, and the sum is zero-free for $\sigma > 1$ (and in the strip its zero-freeness is RH). Where does the template break? For $(a,q) = 1$, $\zeta(s, a/q) = \tfrac{q^s}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi \bmod q}\bar\chi(a)L(s,\chi)$: the coprime Hurwitz functions are the discrete Fourier transform of the $L$-functions, and their joint universality is not free; it is the image of the free joint universality of $(L(\cdot,\chi))_\chi$ under an invertible linear map. The coprime sum inverts the transform and evaluates at the trivial character: $\sum_{(a,q)=1}\zeta(s,a/q) = q^s L(s,\chi_0)$ (the non-coprime $a$ add lower-modulus copies of the same thing). Whatever independent targets $(h_\chi)$ one chooses, the sum lands on $q^s h_{\chi_0}$, and $h_{\chi_0}$ is nonvanishing by the hypothesis of the theorem being used. For $q = 3$ this is completely explicit: $\zeta(s,1/3) + \zeta(s,2/3) = 3^s(1 - 3^{-s})\zeta(s)$, the two Hurwitz summands are $\tfrac{3^s}{2}(L(s,\chi_0) \pm L(s,\chi_{-3}))$, each a Davenport-Heilbronn-type combination, and the template makes either vanish at will while its output for the sum is $3^s h_{\chi_0}$, which it cannot make vanish.

Joint of failure: **the projection onto the one-phase axis lands on a target the tool requires to be nonvanishing.** This is a statement about the template, not about $\zeta$: unpacked, "the sum cannot be made to vanish in the strip" is $L(s,\chi_0) \neq 0$ there, which is RH. The template does not disprove RH here for the same reason it does not prove it: its nonvanishing hypothesis is exactly the sentence at issue.

**2b. The approximate functional equation halves.** $\zeta(s) = \sum_{n \leq x} n^{-s} + \chi(s)\sum_{n \leq y} n^{s-1} + R$ with $xy = t/2\pi$. Two summands, but the second is the functional-equation mirror of the first: same phases $n^{-it}$ read backwards. There is no independent target choice. (Joint of failure: the two summands share one phase system by construction.) The same holds for Hardy's $Z(t)e^{-i\theta(t)}$, and for the split of $\zeta$ into a partial Euler product times its complement, which is a product, not a sum; the tail's convergence in the strip is itself RH (Section 5b), so this split states the question rather than attacking it.

**2c. The functional-equation class of $\zeta$ has no second direction at all.** Hamburger's theorem (1921; Siegel's form): let $f(s) = \sum a_n n^{-s}$ converge absolutely for $\sigma > 1$ with $(s-1)f(s)$ entire of finite order, let $g(s) = \sum b_n n^{-s}$ converge absolutely in some half-plane, and suppose $\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)f(s) = \pi^{-(1-s)/2}\Gamma((1-s)/2)g(1-s)$. Then $f = g = a_1\zeta$. The finite-order hypothesis is load-bearing (it is the growth axiom, not a technicality), and the theorem even allows a different series on the mirror side. The template's input (i) does not exist inside Dirichlet series at conductor 1. In Hecke-group terms: the functional equation plus periodicity is invariance under $\langle z \mapsto z + 1, z \mapsto -1/(Nz)\rangle$, conjugate to the Hecke group $G(\sqrt N)$; at $N = 1$ this is $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, of finite covolume, and the space is one-dimensional. At $N = 5$ and $N = 15$, $\sqrt N > 2$ and the group is Fuchsian of the second kind with infinite covolume: the functional equation alone leaves an infinite-dimensional space of Dirichlet series, and even with the Ramanujan growth axiom (Kaczorowski-Perelli's extended class $\mathcal{S}^\#$) the degree-1 conductor-5 space is a finite-dimensional space of linear combinations of shifted $L(s,\chi)$ with matched functional-equation data, in which Davenport-Heilbronn lives. The Selberg class proper (Euler product added) at degree 1 is $\zeta$ and the shifted $L(s,\chi)$ only (Kaczorowski-Perelli 1999): the Euler-product axiom is what removes every direction, and at conductor 1 the functional equation removes them already.

Joint of failure for 2c: **rigidity.** The disproof has to leave Dirichlet series, or leave conductor 1, to find a deformation direction. Sections 4 and 5a do both.

## 3. The template's output is already refuted for $\zeta$, at Level 3

Suppose one nevertheless found a decomposition satisfying (i)-(ii). The output would be $\gg T$ zeros with $\beta > \sigma_1$ for some $\sigma_1 > 1/2$, i.e. $N(\sigma_1, T) \gg T$. Carlson's density theorem (1920) gives $N(\sigma, T) \ll T^{4\sigma(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon}$, and $4\sigma(1-\sigma) < 1$ for every $\sigma > 1/2$: a contradiction. Note that Bohr-Landau's $N(\sigma,T) = O(T)$ (1914) is NOT enough: Davenport-Heilbronn has $\gg T$ off-line zeros and (conditionally on GRH for the constituents plus a zero-spacing hypothesis, Bombieri-Hejhal 1995) still almost all of its zeros on the line, so $O(T)$ is consistent with the template firing. The refutation needs the strict $o(T)$ of a zero-density estimate: Carlson near $\sigma = 1/2$, Ingham's $3(1-\sigma)/(2-\sigma)$ in mid-strip, Guth-Maynard's $30(1-\sigma)/13$ for $\sigma \geq 3/4$. Every one of these is a mean-value or large-value statement about $\zeta$: a Level-3 fact about proportions.

So the strongest disproof engine is dead on $\zeta$ for a Level-3 reason, and the reason has the same shape as the Level-3 obstruction on the proof side: statistics see proportions, not individuals. Consequence:

**A disproof of RH must be sparse in $T$.** It has to produce an off-line zero without producing $\gg T$ of them; it must live inside the best known density bound at its abscissa. The Bohr / universality family is the only family of mechanisms that has ever produced an off-line zero of anything with an Euler-product-shaped functional equation, and it produces $\gg T$ every time.

## 4. The sparse mechanism: Lehmer pairs, and the pencil where it can be measured

What does a sparse disproof look like at small deformation? Take an object that satisfies (i)-(ii) barely: an Euler product plus $\lambda$ times a second Euler product with the same completed functional equation, $\lambda \to 0$. For $d = -15$, $h(d) = 2$, $d = (-3)(5)$:

$$A(s) = \zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{-15}),\qquad B(s) = L(s,\chi_{-3})L(s,\chi_5),\qquad f_\lambda = A + \lambda B.$$

Both complete to $\Lambda(s) = 15^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\,(\cdot)(s) = \Lambda(1-s)$ with root number $+1$ (Legendre duplication on $\Gamma(s/2)\Gamma(\tfrac{s+1}{2})$; all three characters real primitive), so $Z_\lambda(t) := \Lambda_\lambda(1/2 + it)$ is real for every real $\lambda$, and zeros leave the line only in pairs $(\rho, 1 - \bar\rho)$. Every member has the same simple pole at $s = 1$ (residue $L(1,\chi_{-15})$; $B$ is entire). The endpoints: $f_0 = A$ is an Euler product (RH for $f_0$ is RH plus GRH for $\chi_{-15}$); $f_{\pm 1}$ are the Epstein zeta functions of the principal and non-principal forms of discriminant $-15$ (checked coefficient by coefficient to $n = 200$: $r_{Q_0}(n) = a_A(n) + a_B(n)$, $r_{Q_1}(n) = a_A(n) - a_B(n)$, no unit factor); $f_\infty = B$ is the other Euler product. Voronin gives $\gg T$ off-line zeros for every $\lambda \neq 0$. By the pole-allowing form of Weil's converse theorem (twists by primitive characters over infinitely many moduli coprime to 15, with Gauss-sum root numbers, and vertical-strip boundedness), the span $\{\alpha A + \beta B\}$ is the full two-dimensional Eisenstein space of weight-1 forms of level 15 and character $\chi_{-15}$ (the unordered primitive pairs with product $\chi_{-15}$ and conductor product dividing 15 are exactly $\{1, \chi_{-15}\}$ and $\{\chi_{-3}, \chi_5\}$; $S_1(\Gamma_1(N)) = 0$ for $N \leq 22$): the complete space of Dirichlet series with this functional equation AND its twists. The functional equation alone leaves the infinite-dimensional Hecke-group space of Section 2c. The pencil is the natural finite-dimensional slice through both Euler products, and this is the point of choosing it: on the projective line of the pencil the two Euler products are two points, and the conjecture is that the RH-true set is exactly those two points.

**The Lehmer-pair model.** At small $\lambda$ the universality zeros sit at astronomical heights; the zeros that leave the line at accessible heights do so by a local mechanism. Near two adjacent simple zeros $t_1 < t_2$ of $Z_0$ with midpoint $t_m$ and gap $\delta$, $Z_0(t) \approx c[(t - t_m)^2 - \delta^2/4]$ with $c = Z_0''(t_m)/2$, and $Z_\lambda = Z_0 + \lambda Z_B$. The pair's two zeros satisfy $(t - t_m)^2 = \delta^2/4 - \lambda Z_B(t_m)/c$ and become a complex-conjugate pair exactly when $\lambda Z_B(t_m)/c > \delta^2/4$:

$$\operatorname{sign}\lambda_c = \operatorname{sign}\big(Z_B(t_m)Z_0''(t_m)\big),\qquad |\lambda_c| \approx \frac{|Z_0''(t_m)|}{8\,|Z_B(t_m)|}\,\delta^2.$$

The prefactor is pair-dependent, so the $\delta^2$ law is a statement about the model's ratio, tested as $\lambda_c/\lambda_{\rm pred}$, not as a raw log-log slope. The same computation for the de Bruijn-Newman flow, where the deformation direction is $-Z_0''$ itself (since $\partial_t H_t = -\partial_z^2 H_t$), has a universal prefactor and a collision at $t = -\delta^2/8$; it is only the two-zero caricature of that flow (the real flow moves every zero at once, and Rodgers-Tao's theorem is a statement about the whole ensemble, Section 5a). In the pencil, the caricature is the whole story at low height, which is why the threshold is measurable pair by pair.

## 5. The other disproof roads, each run to its stop

**5a. The heat-flow direction (de Bruijn-Newman).** $H_t(z) = \int_0^\infty e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du$, $H_0 = \xi$ up to normalization; RH $\iff \Lambda \leq 0$. Rodgers-Tao (2020): $\Lambda \geq 0$; Polymath 15 (2019): $\Lambda \leq 0.22$. So RH $\iff \Lambda = 0$, and a disproof is $\Lambda > 0$: a pair that is already complex at $t = 0$. The Rodgers-Tao argument assumes $\Lambda < 0$, which implies RH and so makes RH-conditional inputs available, and shows that the backward flow would leave the zeros of $\xi$ in a configuration too regular to be consistent with those inputs. It is one-sided by construction: at $t = 0$ nothing is contradicted, and forward flow only repels. The only certificate of $\Lambda > 0$ is a complex zero of $H_0$, i.e. the number. The mechanism reaches the boundary from the bad side and stops there. (Repo probe of the same flow on the D-H control: [`e_dbn_flow_dh.md`](../../experiments/criticality/e_dbn_flow_dh.md).)

**5b. Euler-product divergence.** The partial Euler products $\prod_{p \leq x}(1 - p^{-s})^{-1}$ converge locally uniformly to $\zeta(s)$ throughout $\sigma > 1/2$ if and only if RH (forward by partial summation on $\pi(x) = \mathrm{li}(x) + O(\sqrt{x}\log x)$; reverse by Hurwitz on the nonvanishing partial products). So $\Theta := \sup\beta > 1/2$ is the same statement as the product's failure to converge in $\sigma > \Theta - \varepsilon$, equivalently (Landau's oscillation theorem) $\psi(x) - x = \Omega_\pm(x^{\Theta - \varepsilon})$. Three coordinate systems for the same sentence; a disproof in any one of them is the number in disguise, and the primes thread ([`PRIME_PATTERNS.md`](../../experiments/primes/PRIME_PATTERNS.md), every prime to $10^{13}$) shows the prime-side coordinate is the least efficient of the three at surfacing it.

**5c. Negativity in the positivity criteria.** RH $\iff \lambda_n \geq 0$ for all $n$ (Li) $\iff$ Weil's form is positive on Schwartz functions. A disproof is one negative $\lambda_n$ or one negative Weil value. The repo's Architecture-3 thread has been the search for that negative value from the proof side: the Schur-complement detector of probe `e3l` fires on D-H (4 negative eigenvalues, one per off-line height below 200) and on the non-principal Epstein form of $d = -47$ (1; its off-line zero is re-certified by winding number this session, see the dossier), and on nothing for $\zeta$; the Li coefficients of that Epstein function go negative from $n = 110{,}000$ (probe `e3b4`) while $\zeta$'s follow Bombieri-Lagarias $\tfrac{n}{2}\log n + cn$ with positive margin; and the soft-positivity margin for $\zeta$ is measured at zero-ish but never negative (the compass of the stance section of CLAUDE.md). An off-line zero at height $\gamma$ contributes to $\lambda_n$ only once $n \gtrsim \gamma^2/(\beta - \tfrac12)$ (the partner with $|1 - 1/\rho| \approx 1 + (\tfrac12 - \beta)/|\rho|^2 > 1$ grows like $\exp(n(\beta - \tfrac12)/\gamma^2)$), so at $\gamma > 3\times10^{12}$ this is $n > 10^{25}$: the positivity coordinate hides the number even better than the primes do.

**5d. Statistics.** GUE agreement, Selberg's CLT, the multifractal structure: all provably RH-blind (move a zero off the line, every statistic is unchanged bit for bit; PRIME_PATTERNS Sections 5c-5d, and LEARNINGS #174's axiom census for why duality, integrality and the Euler product buy only the trivial region). No statistical disproof exists even in principle.

**5e. Refuted consequences.** The non-constructive template "RH $\Rightarrow X$, $\neg X$" with $X$ any computable consequence: Robin's inequality $\sigma(n) < e^{\gamma}n\log\log n$ for $n > 5040$, Lagarias's harmonic-number form, $M(x) = O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})$, Lindelöf. Each has been searched (colossally abundant numbers for Robin; the Mertens computations that refuted $|M(x)| < \sqrt x$ without touching RH) and each violation, if one exists, sits at an $n$ or $x$ governed by the same hiding law as 5b: the consequence coordinate is a re-encoding of the zero's height.

**5f. Logic.** An independence proof would prove RH true ($\Pi^0_1$). There is no logical route to $\neg$RH that does not guarantee a witness.

By Voronin no class-number $\geq 2$ Epstein function is RH-true; the only RH-true objects in these families are the Euler products themselves, which is the pencil's lesson stated for the whole control set.

## 7. What the negative proof taught (instrument reading, not graded)

Every disproof road converges on one object, an off-line zero above height $3\times10^{12}$, and every analytic accelerator stops at the same place, for the same reason, from either side:

1. **The $\gg T$ engine is dead at Level 3.** The Bohr / universality family plus Rouché is the only family of mechanisms that has ever produced an off-line zero of a functional-equation object, its output is $\gg T$ zeros, and a zero-density estimate refutes that for $\zeta$ by a mean-value or large-value argument. A disproof must be sparse in $T$; no sparse mechanism is known for anything.
2. **Every decomposition of $\zeta$ into independent summands loses independence at the projection onto the trivial character, and the loss is a hypothesis of the tool.** The Euler products are the coordinate axes of the character basis; vanishing-universality lives off the axes; the sum over a Hurwitz or character decomposition is the projection back onto the axis, where the tool's nonvanishing hypothesis is the sentence at issue. This is the Davenport-Heilbronn discipline stated in linear algebra: a sum can vanish, a product cannot where it converges, and $\zeta$ is a product whose convergence in the strip is RH.
3. **$\zeta$ is rigid (Hamburger) and sits at the boundary of the disproof region in every deformation direction that exists.** Heat flow: $\Lambda \geq 0$, boundary from the bad side. The Euler pencil: every $\lambda \neq 0$ is bad (Voronin), boundary from the bad side, with the approach law measured in Section 6. Both are open-condition (stability) mechanisms: they certify membership in the open bad set for perturbed points and are silent at the boundary point itself.
4. **Read as a proof coordinate:** RH is a closed condition (Hurwitz's theorem: a locally uniform limit, not identically zero, of functions with all zeros on the line has all zeros on the line), and every disproof mechanism is an open condition. A stability argument cannot certify a boundary point's membership in the closed set. The proof side has exactly two non-stability routes, and both are known programs: approximate $\xi$ from INSIDE the closed set (Pólya / Laguerre-Pólya / Lee-Yang; the de Bruijn-Newman flow at large $t$ does this, and RH is the statement that the approximation reaches $t = 0$), or certify the closed condition directly (a positivity, M4). The disproof side has only the witness. This is the negative image of the all-roads convergence ([`all_roads_to_the_signature.md`](all_roads_to_the_signature.md)): all disproof roads converge on a number, all proof roads converge on a positivity or a limit from inside, and the convergences meet at the same object because the number and the positivity are the same $\Pi^0_1$ sentence read from its two sides.
5. **What is not obviously derivable from the standing clauses and might survive the #201 check:** the boundary-point framing (3, 4) as a statement about deformation families rather than about the explicit-formula budget, and the measured approach law of Section 6. Whether these are a re-measurement of the wall in new words is the deliberation's call, not this session's.

The disproof was built honestly and it broke where every known disproof mechanism breaks on $\zeta$: the mechanisms are $\gg T$ in output and stability-type in logic, $\zeta$'s density theorems refuse the first and its boundary position refuses the second, and what remains is the witness. Whether that witness exists is RH; this exercise did not decide it, and did not pretend to. What it leaves behind is a sharper statement of why the proof side cannot be a stability argument either.
